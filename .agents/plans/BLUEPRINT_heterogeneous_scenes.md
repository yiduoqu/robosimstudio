# BLUEPRINT — heterogeneous scenes (Clawhauser on a rod, Clawhauser into a bottle)

**Oracle**: `.agents/plans/ORACLE_heterogeneous_scenes.md` holds the approved plan
verbatim; it is the authority for scope, rules and deliverables. This blueprint
turns it into an ordered, verifiable feature list. Deviations are recorded in the
*Deviation log* below, with the evidence that forced them.

## Goal and interpreted intent

Simulate the existing labelled heterogeneous Clawhauser FEM (parts 0 tail, 1 body,
2 loop) in two new scenes with the current RSS/Newton pipeline — hung on a
horizontal rod through its loop, and stuffed into a glass cylinder too small for
it — and report what the simulation can and cannot do, with videos. Reaching the
scenes needs two small physics/actuation fixes the previous branch already
qualified: a coarse common-translation corrector (the stiff FEM cannot balance its
own weight at 12 iterations) and a force-bounded claw (the current claw prescribes
poses, so contact force is unbounded and unmeasurable). Both are re-implemented
concise and checked for parity against the branch's own code on the same scenes.

## Requirements

1. Two runnable examples: `examples/10_heterogeneous/clawhauser_on_rod.py`, `clawhauser_stuff_into_bottle.py`, same style and CLI as `clawhauser_pick_place.py`, rendering a 30 fps video plus `result.json` / diagnostics.
2. Corrector parity (§7 of the oracle): same hook point, terms, budget and refusal, minus the documented omissions.
3. Claw parity: same PD law, gains, clamp order, pad geometry, mass formula, velocity semantics.
4. `collide=False` must actually drop particle collision.
5. Quality instrument: substep-latched per-part min J, inversions, nonfinite, penetration.
6. One new test module; the existing examples keep working unchanged.
7. Videos + a results README section with the observed limits.

## Assumptions

- Newton pinned at `third-party/newton` `454fc3e` (`hanson/dino-twist`); the branch's `vbd_coarse.py` is written against that commit, so its Newton calls port directly.
- The branch's qualified setting is a 20 mm contact query gap with a 5 mm per-substep correction budget; 0.25 mm budgets fail.
- Clawhauser mass ≈ 1.227 N in this scene; the branch's resting control reports ≈1.238 N support.
- The branch's numbers come from its own runs; our parity A/B reproduces them under our harness rather than trusting the report.

## Non-goals

Everything in oracle §6 under *Not written or ported*: `parallel_gripper`, `cord_grasp`/`cord_policy`, `trajectory`, contact recording/determinism, `check_gripper`, reset/benchmark machinery, material overrides, multi-world/marker/reset work, the rewritten pick-and-place example, the two long branch documents. Also out of scope: real-robot evidence, material fitting, and any change to the source mesh/materials.

## Features (dependency order)

### W1 — coarse common-translation corrector (blocks all scene work)
- **Purpose**: make the stiff FEM able to balance its own weight and external load.
- **Deliverable**: `robosimstudio_materials/coarse_translation.py`, opt-in via dotted backend path, wired through the existing material-backend seam only.
- **Prerequisites**: none.
- **Acceptance**: resting control on the real volume — support ≈ weight (≈1.238 N vs 1.227 N), zero inverted substeps, no energy gain; budget pair 20 mm/5 mm passes and 0.25 mm fails; plain VBD at the same substeps/iterations is the negative control; A/B against the branch's `vbd_coarse.py` on one scene agrees on COM path, support load, min J and inversions within a stated tolerance.
- **Plan file**: `PLAN_coarse_translation.md` (includes W3 + W5, which ride with it).
- **Risk**: the concise port may not reach the branch's numbers; mitigation = control + A/B before scene work; fallback to the reviewed `vbd_coarse.py` only with the user's say-so.

### W2 — force-bounded claw (blocks grasp-dependent scene work)
- **Purpose**: contact force becomes a bounded, measurable quantity; kills the overcompression the branch traced to prescribed poses.
- **Deliverable**: `examples/10_heterogeneous/gripper.py` gains `mode="dynamic"` with clamped PD wrench on the existing finger bodies, plus `pad_half_width` and `ledge` fields.
- **Prerequisites**: none (independent of W1).
- **Acceptance**: `mode="kinematic"` default unchanged and the existing example regresses clean on the HPC; dynamic mode applies ≤5 N / ≤0.03 N·m with gravity compensation inside the same budget; A/B against the branch gripper on one scene with identical waypoints agrees on applied wrench, peak force/torque, finger poses and object outcome.
- **Plan file**: `PLAN_gripper_dynamic.md` (includes W4).
- **W2b (force split, added after audit)**: closing and pressing are different budgets. `force_limit` (5 N) bounds every grasp; `push_force_limit` (50 N) with its own torque limit and gains is selected by the claw's own `set_press` state, not passed per call site. Acceptance: a press measured above the 5 N closing clamp with the mesh intact; the grasp peak still ≤5 N.

### W3 — `collide=False` also clears particle collision
- **Deliverable**: one line in `robosimstudio/scene/entity.py`, byte-identical to the branch.
- **Acceptance**: a `collide=False` decor slab generates no particle contacts (covered in W5's module).

### W4 — mesh quality instrument
- **Deliverable**: `examples/10_heterogeneous/mesh_quality.py` (~60 lines): substep-latched per-part min J, inversion/nonfinite counts, penetration measure.
- **Acceptance**: same definitions as oracle §7; A/B against the branch module on one run.

### W5 — one test module
- **Deliverable**: `tests/test_coarse_translation.py` — resting balance, budget clamp, self-contact/DAT refusal, and the `collide=False` two-case check.
- **Acceptance**: passes in the fast tier (HPC); nothing else added.

### Scene A — `clawhauser_on_rod.py`
- **Deliverable**: scene per oracle §9 — slab, post, horizontal rod through the loop; narrow-pad dynamic grasp at the loop apex; waypoint path settle → close → lift → thread → seat → hold → open → withdraw; video + results.
- **Prerequisites**: W1, W2, W3, W4.
- **Acceptance**: oracle §9 gates (rod threaded; ≥20 mm body clearance through the hold; zero finger contact after release; min J > 0; penetration ≤0.5 mm; peak force ≤5 N, torque ≤0.03 N·m; support load vs 1.227 N; loop stretch; seated depth) plus the kinematic and plain-VBD controls for contrast.
- **Commit** before Scene B starts.

### Scene B — `clawhauser_stuff_into_bottle.py`
- **Deliverable**: scene per oracle §10 — 24-panel faceted glass cylinder, inner Ø60 mm, decor non-colliding; bounded pads pinch the body, transport over the mouth, descend, hold, open, withdraw; stop reason recorded.
- **Prerequisites**: W1, W2, W3, W4; shares the harness Scene A builds.
- **Acceptance**: oracle §10 (max insertion depth, inside fraction, wedged after release?, jaw effort vs deformation, penetration ≤0.5 mm, no inverted/nonfinite substeps); knob sweeps reported with measured effects.

### Docs and reporting
- `examples/10_heterogeneous/README.md` section: settings, run recipe, observed limits.
- `ROBOSIMSTUDIO_PLAN.md` updated with status; final report linking videos and result files.

## Integration and validation strategy

- Corrector and claw are validated in isolation (controls + A/B parity) before any scene consumes them; the untouched kinematic path and `clawhauser_pick_place.py` guard against regressions.
- Every scene run is a submitted HPC job; artifacts are pulled back and looked at (video stills, diagnostics), not inferred from exit codes.
- The complete outcome is validated by: both scene videos showing the intended interaction, their `result.json` gates passing, and the limits section reporting measured numbers with the plain-VBD control as the counterfactual.
- **Independence**: only web-research subagents exist in this environment, so no independent implementer/validator reviews the port. Parity A/B against the branch's own implementations is the substitute evidence; the report says so.

## Deviation log

**D17 — the press is the same path at a stiffer spring, and the button force is reachable.** The budget was decoration: the carrier's PD gives `ke * lag`, and at 1500 N/m over the 85 mm stroke the press topped out at 127 N, so 50, 200 and 400 N all measured ~60 N. Three actuators were measured before one worked. (i) *Rate damper*, `10 kN*s/m * (guide_rate - carrier_rate)`: unstable -- 10 kN*s/m against 0.3 kg at the 1.67 ms substep is about 28x the explicit-damping limit, so it rang, pinned `peak_push_force_n` at the budget for the whole run and never followed the withdraw. (ii) *Commanded force*, `-budget + one-sided stop`: the stop was a 1500 N/m spring, which 200 N overran by 20-40 mm -- the pads drove through the toy (min J -3.5, 27 inverted tets, 94-99% 'inside' because the body was smeared through the bore) and the claw never released. (iii) *Stiff press spring*: pressing uses 50 kN/m, carrying keeps 1500. The pads follow the commanded path -- the same 20 mm/s descent, the same 85 mm travel -- and the force saturates at the budget within 4 mm of lag. Measured: 200 N budget -> 145.4-200.0 N peak, 53.0-57.8% of the volume inside, 363-371 contact frames, 0.60-0.99 mm into the shell, 0-1 inverted tets, all three released and wedged; 100 N budget -> exactly 100.0 N, 48.1-59.0% inside, 0.25-0.60 mm, 1 of 3 released. The judge's 0.5 mm shell gate refuses the 200 N set: the toy is 1.2 N and 0.5 MPa, so 200 N is what pushes it through the glass, and the clean press remains the 63 N the old soft spring happened to deliver (`outputs/bottle/v1-3-dyn`, 46.1-48.2% inside, 0.12-0.43 mm). The 60 N is a consequence of the soft spring, not a setting.

Also in this round, and kept: the claw re-aims after the release. The handover used to build the whole approach from the body centre measured at the stall, which is stale by the time the toy has fallen onto the rim; `after_jam` now only opens and holds, and `chase_path` re-measures the body's top where it actually is and slides over it before descending. It is gated on no contact yet, because a run whose release already left the pads on the body is the press's and re-aiming there drags them across the top they are standing on.

**D16 — the wall's friction, the push ceiling and a wider bore are all measured dead ends.** Three answers to "the toy still is not deforming into the cup", each three runs at 24 s and 720 steps with video.

(i) *Low wall friction.* `CUP_FRICTION = 0.02` on the container's floor and panels (`sqrt(2.0 * 0.02) = 0.2` after VBD's mix; the pads keep `sqrt(2.0 * 4.0) = 2.8`) gives 0 of 6: p200a-c and p400a-c all report `object-dropped`, 25-35% inside, three of them 0.75 m below the bench. The rim's friction is what holds the body on the mouth while it is squeezed; with a frictionless wall the 10 mm interference has a normal force and no tangential resistance, so the toy slides sideways off the rim instead of in. Reverted.

(ii) *Doubling the push clamp.* The clamp was never binding: the carrier's press force is its 1500 N/m spring's lag behind the commanded path, so the 85 mm stroke caps the press at 127 N and 50, 200 and 400 N all measured the same 60.5 N peak. With the carriage stiffened to 5000 N/m the budget becomes reachable -- r200a 67.9 / r200b 161.6 / r400a 84.7 / r400b 60.1 N -- and the body is pressed 49.6-55.2% inside instead of 46.1-48.2%, but it tears: 1 of 3 clean at 200 N (0.59-1.29 mm into the wall against the 0.5 mm gate) and 1 of 3 at 400 N, and the frames show the pads inside a shredded body. 400 N buys nothing over 200 N. Reverted to 1500 N/m.

(iii) *A 105 mm bore*, to halve the width the body must lose. 0 of 3: b105a-c settle at 76-81 mm with `press_contact_frames` 0-1 (`no-body-contact`, `object-dropped`), because a lightly held body slides past the rim on its own before the pads arrive. 100 mm is the widest bore that keeps the body up where the press can reach it.

The limit is geometric: the 110 mm body must lose 10 mm of width to pass a 100 mm bore, ~5 mm of that is available at 60 N of contact, and the force that would buy the rest tears the mesh. 100 mm, 200 N, the soft carriage and the wall's own friction are the configuration that works -- 3 of 3, `outputs/bottle/v1-3-dyn`.

**D15 — the push clamp, not the press path, was stalling the press: 200 N replaces 50 N.** The same scene at 24 s and 720 steps, three runs per cap. 50 N -- s1/s2/s3, 2 of 3 clean; s2 peaked at exactly 50.0 N (the clamp) and s1 pressed 97 mm and reported `released: false`. 100 N -- u1/u2/u3, 1 of 3 clean; u2 and u3 stuck at 48.1 and 72.1 N, 0.1 and 1.4 mm of press depth. 200 N -- v1/v2/v3, 3 of 3 clean: 85.3 mm insertion, 46.1-48.2% of the volume inside, 401-403 frames of body contact, peaks 63.0 / 60.5 / 9.5 N, all below the clamp, 0.12-0.67 mm into the container's shell, 0.56-0.67 mm of pad indentation, wedged after release, one transient tet inversion in v1 reported as `local_inversion`. At 200 N the 85 mm path ends the press; at 50 N the carrier lagged it, the body did not travel with the pads, and the relative withdraw left the pads inside the plush. The grasp clamp is unchanged at 5 N. `--push-cap` defaults and `hpc/submit_bottle.sh` move with it; the runs stay on disk as `outputs/bottle/{s,u,v}1-3-dyn`.

**D14 — the cup press is bounded at 85 mm and releases while it lifts.** The claw's trajectory advances one pose per two control frames: the 20 mm/s press descent measures 0.33 mm/frame against a 0.667 mm/frame command, and the 30 mm/s approach 18.8 mm/s against 30. So a path to the container's floor -- 180 mm from the body's top -- is still descending when the 24 s run ends. p1-p9 all reported `released: false` with the carrier drive at its -50 N clamp on the last frame; none of them ever reached its own open or rise, and the earlier reading that the claw was pinned by the plush was that unfinished path, not a measured grip. Three changes follow. (i) *Bounded press.* `press_path` drives 85 mm below the pose the pads first touch the body at, and never past 6 mm above the floor. Measured: 45 mm -- r1 4.77 N, r2 3.77 N (refused, the push gate needs more than the 5 N grasp clamp), r3 12.85 N clean; 85 mm -- s1 13.78 N but `released: false` after 97 mm of travel (the press past its path, stuck in the body, 7 transient / 3 settled inverted tets), s2 50.0 N clean, s3 10.34 N clean. 2 of 3 at 85 mm. (ii) *Open while lifting.* Opening at full load left p4-p6 at a 34 mm half-gap for 100 frames: 5 N of jaw cannot slide out from under a 50 N normal at mu 2. (iii) *Two penetrations.* The 0.5 mm gate read every soft contact, including the pad's indentation into the plush (0.51 mm at 50 N), which is the deformation the press exists to produce. It now tests `max_container_penetration_m`; `max_pad_penetration_m` reports the indentation, and `max_penetration_m` keeps the old all-contact number. `inserted` is 90% of the usable depth, because the compressed body leaves the tail a few millimetres short of a rigid floor. Geometry, clamps and bore are unchanged. Video `outputs/bottle/s2-dyn/overview.mp4`: the pads land on the body's top, drive it 57.6 mm into the mouth at the 50 N clamp, the body bulges over the rim, the claw opens and lifts clear, and the toy stays wedged at 85.3 mm.

**D1 — Scene A's grasp point is the loop's widest point, not its apex cord.** The oracle (§9) asks for the pads on the loop's apex cord, hole free. Measured on the first build: the pads close on the arch above the hole, where the outer surface is nearly horizontal, so the contact normals point up — the applied wrenches at frame 46 are `[1.24, −0.06, 1.44]` and `[−1.43, −0.01, 0.79]`, i.e. the pads press *down* with more force than they pinch — and the pads then ride over the cord and off it: 224 finger contacts through the close, 0 from the first lifted step, ring centre z unchanged within 1 mm, toy minimum z unchanged at 0.7507, `rod_threaded false`, `success false`. The loop's apex is a beam a pad cannot pinch from the side. The grasp moved to the loop's outer sides at the hole's own centre height — its widest point, and the only place on a closed loop where the outer surface faces the closing direction — keeping the oracle's intent (narrow pads, no ledge, hole free, bounded force, hole free for the rod) and adding a fixed closing interference so the squeeze is the 5 N clamp rather than whatever the loop's compliance leaves. Recorded before the corrected run was read.

**D3 — Scene A seats the toy on the rod before the jaws open, and withdraws straight up.** Two motion details moved, both from measured failures of earlier builds. (i) *Seating.* Driving the pads down to the touching height pressed the loop onto the rod with the claw's whole clamp — measured 7.03 N against a 1.227 N weight, with the body's worst tet reaching J −0.503 — and the loop returned that as a bounce: the toy ended held clear of the rod and dropped past it when the jaws opened. Threading with more margin (12 mm nominal instead of 4; the loop's own sag ate the first 4) and coming down onto the rod with the jaws still closed leaves the rod carrying the toy at the hold, which is the interaction §9 asks for. (ii) *Withdrawal.* The withdraw moved the pads 20 mm across while rising, and a build that stopped the seat 4 mm above the rod left the toy hanging free of the rod at that moment: the open jaws swept it with them — measured as the loop's centroid travelling 75 mm in x and 34 mm up *with the fingers*, nothing else touching the toy (`finger_contacts` 3, rod load 0.000 N) at the end of a run whose earlier frames had it threaded cleanly. The jaws now leave upwards only.

**D4 — the pad-particle contact path is not bit-reproducible, so Scene A is judged by repeated runs.** Two identical runs of the same build (jobs 4521831 and 4521844) diverge at frame 34 — the first frame the pads touch the loop — by 3.6e-7 m in the loop's centroid, and the seating, which is a contact-rich bistable event, lands differently: one run seated the toy on the rod (settled rod load 1.08–1.44 N against the 1.227 N weight) and one bounced it clear and dropped it past the rod. The corrector's own reductions are deterministic (validated bit-identical against the branch); Newton's per-contact penalty ramping for body-particle contacts is not, and making it so is outside this mission's scope. Consequence: Scene A's acceptance is three identical runs all ending with the toy hanging on the rod, and §11's limits report states the nondeterminism with this measurement rather than claiming a reproducible trajectory.

**D5 — Scene A's grasp bite and rod engagement are measured, not nominal.** Two numbers that the scene states from the mesh were re-derived because the first values failed in runs. *Bite.* Closing 1 mm past the loop's measured width presses about 1 N per pad, and the cord — round where the pads meet it — slides back out of the pinch on the first lifted step: five finger contacts through the close, none from the lift on, the toy's minimum z unmoved at the bench, in three identical runs. 6 mm holds through lift, thread and seat at the 5 N clamp. *Engagement.* Threading so that the rod's tip only just clears the loop's near face leaves 21 mm of rod in the hole; the seated loop then walked 20 mm along the rod in the pinch and off the tip, losing the rod (rod load 0.000 N from the end of the seat onward, the toy then carried up 29 mm by the withdrawing jaws). The scene now threads 36 mm inboard. *Tail.* The run is 8 s rather than 7.5 so the last half second is still and the settled window the hang is judged on is quiet.

**D9 — the claw gets the archive's carrier, and the flat pads are measured to drop the loop.** The archive branch's `parallel_gripper.py` builds a 120 x 30 x 18 mm carrier 45 mm above two 6 x 12 x 12 mm flat fingers; `gripper.py` had only the fingers, so the claw read as two rectangles. `ScriptedGripperSpec` now takes `carrier=True`, `carrier_half_extents` and `carrier_offset_z`; the carrier is a kinematic, colliding body whose pose is set each substep to the fingers' midpoint (`_place_carrier`). It adds no drive axis and no measured force. Both scenes use it. The pads were also tried flat (no ledge) at the user's request: 8 mm squeeze, mu 2.0, 90 mm bore (g90, m90) -- the loop rides 87-95 mm down the grip during the carry, the toy falls into the cup, and the fingers end above the body with nothing to press. The 2.4 mm ledge is load-bearing and the bottle keeps it; the rod was already ledge-free and still works because the rod takes the weight within 2 s.

**D12 — the both-pad press does not close the body's rim limit.** The audit asked to load both pads and stop the push torque saturating. The re-centre over the measured body centre (the pads were 17 mm in x and 41 mm in y off in p2) loads both: q1 12 mm pads, 8 mm press -- 5.67 / 10.2 N, min J -1.05; q2 40 mm archive face, 8 mm -- 14.94 / 14.30 N, min J -1.08 with 2156 inverted; q3 40 mm, 4 mm -- 21.4 / 13.7 N, min J -0.94, the toy pushed 10.4 mm then expelled to the bench (0.5% inside). All refused. The 40 mm face also found two real defects: at the old 90 mm opening its corners touched the 100 mm wall (119 finger-container contacts; the opening is now 80 mm, q3 0), and a toy resting on the container floor was called dropped by the table-clearance test (`on_bench` now also requires it outside the bore). `press_depth` is measured from first contact, not the jam: q2's 61.7 mm was the post-jam fall, not the press. The local WSL runs are 1.67-1.70 s/frame, 450 steps in 12.5 min, so the machine is not the limit. **D11 — the press is a contact-guarded push with its own force budget.** Supersedes D10's path and numbers.
